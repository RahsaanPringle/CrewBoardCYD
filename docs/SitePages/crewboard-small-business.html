Built around the work, not the office

A successful lawncare business doesn't need a corporate inbox for every mower.

A business can grow through reliable work, good equipment, and a dependable crew without ever needing an email server or a corporate email address for each employee.

But the need to coordinate is still there. Owners need one place for the plan. Crew members need to know where they're going, who they're working with, and what changed.

You don't need to turn every worker into an office worker to keep everyone on the same page.
  • Where are we working today?A shared schedule with job sites and assigned crew.
  • Who's coming with me?A roster and crew assignments people can check themselves.
  • Did the plan change?A message board with named posts and replies.
  • What do I need to know about this site?Job notes and saved business locations.

CrewBoard is not a smaller email system. It's a different tool for these everyday questions.

Local means local. The current device is a shop-side coordination hub, not a mobile dispatch service. Crew members cannot check it from a job site unless they are connected to the network that can reach it. There are no push notifications; people open or refresh the board to see updates.

Right-sized, not all-purpose

A small server for a business that would never buy a traditional one.

A good conversation to have if...

  • Your team starts or ends the day at a shared shop, yard, or small premises.
  • You need shared schedules and updates, not an inbox for every employee.
  • You want the core information stored on a device at your business.
  • You have a small team and someone who can own setup and backups.

A different tool is needed if...

  • Your crew needs live dispatch and updates from anywhere over cellular data.
  • You need payroll, invoicing, route optimization, or a full customer-management system.
  • You need enterprise security, high availability, or many simultaneous users.
  • You need sensitive records managed with strong access controls and encrypted connections.

The questions an owner should ask

Simple doesn't have to mean vague.

Do my employees need company email addresses?

No. CrewBoard uses local usernames and passwords. It does not host email, create mailboxes, or require an email subscription for each employee.

Does this replace Microsoft Exchange or Microsoft 365?

No. Those products solve broader email and office-collaboration needs. CrewBoard is for a narrower job: helping a small crew share a plan without adopting corporate email in the first place.

Does it need the internet?

The core board runs on the local network without an internet connection. Phones still need to reach the CYD over Wi-Fi. The optional street-map background and external map links need internet on the viewing device.

Where does the information live?

On a microSD card in the device, rather than in a required hosted service. Backups are manual: someone needs to copy the card's data and protect the copies. Local storage does not remove the need for backups or careful handling of employee information.

How much IT does it take?

There is no conventional server operating system or email platform to administer, but this is not maintenance-free. Someone still handles Wi-Fi setup, device updates, user accounts, and backups. Packaging those tasks as an installation or support service is a possible offering, not an existing included service.

Is it ready to be sold as a fully supported appliance?

This page describes a working prototype and its product opportunity, not a production-readiness guarantee. The current build uses unencrypted HTTP and is intended only for a trusted private network. Do not expose it directly to the internet or use it for sensitive records. Production security, durability, support, and deployment requirements need to be established before a commercial rollout.

What's the price?

Hardware, installation, and support pricing have not been set. The current core workflow does not require a cloud service or employee email subscriptions; that is an architectural choice, not a promise of free hardware, setup, or ongoing support.
