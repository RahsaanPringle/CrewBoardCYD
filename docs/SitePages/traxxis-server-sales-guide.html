Traxxis Server

A friendly digital front desk for a small team: find employee contacts, check upcoming time off, and print the forms the business already uses.

Useful local tools for businesses that like keeping paperwork simple.

A simple place for everyday business information

Traxxis Server gives a small team one private place on its business network to check who works there, see upcoming time off, and find printable forms. It brings a few useful digital tools to owners who want to keep employee paperwork on paper.

Start with the daily routine: an employee needs a contact, the owner wants to see who is off next week, or someone needs a blank form. Traxxis Server puts those basics together in one place that staff can reach from a phone or computer on the business Wi-Fi.

Made for small, paper-friendly businesses

Traxxis Server is designed for owners who want a shared digital reference point while keeping familiar paper processes. A small landscaping company or family-run battery shop can make practical use of staff contacts, a time-off calendar, and printable forms without changing how people fill out paperwork.

Right-sized space, with a path to grow

The starter offer pairs a 32 GB microSD card with 20 GB of advertised shared-file space. About 2 GB is set aside for the server software and apps, with additional headroom for smooth day-to-day operation.

A natural first step: put blank forms where employees can find and print them. As the business's information needs grow, the server can become a starting point for a broader conversation about organizing business data.

Private by design: on the business network

Traxxis Server is intended to live on the business's existing router and serve people using that local network. Individual employee, administrator, and owner passwords make access personal, while the core apps remain centered on the workplace rather than a cloud account.

This local approach gives small businesses a familiar, on-site home for routine information. HTTPS/TLS is a planned layer to evaluate for encrypting browser traffic before launch; we will describe only protections that have been implemented and verified.

Privacy-friendly workflow: the shared library is for blank forms and ordinary business references. Employees print and complete sensitive paperwork on paper, then return it through the company's existing process.

Focused on the small-business essentials

Traxxis Server is purpose-built around the tasks that make an immediate difference for a small local team:

  • Workplace access: open the apps on the business Wi-Fi with role-based employee, administrator, and owner accounts.
  • Paper-first documents: publish blank forms and references for employees to view, print, and complete through familiar paper processes.
  • Clear daily coordination: start with employee contacts, vacations, and holidays; grow into work scheduling as the business is ready.
  • Owner-controlled storage: keep the shared library on the SD card and make a manual backup by powering down and copying the card.
  • Room to expand: begin with 20 GB of shared file space and discuss a tested expansion or broader data solution as needs grow.

For businesses that later need remote access, online HR or payroll, electronic form submission, advanced approvals, or higher-volume document work, Traxxis Server can be the first step in a broader data-management plan.

Trust is part of the product: keep completed employee forms and other sensitive personal paperwork out of the shared library. Treat HTTPS/TLS as a readiness item and make encryption claims only after the feature is implemented and tested.

A simple conversation to guide a demo

  1. Begin with their routine. “How do employees find the right form or find out who is off next week?”
  2. Connect the need to an app. Show contacts in the roster, upcoming leave in Schedule, or a blank form in Document Share.
  3. Demonstrate the paper-friendly path. Click the document, preview or download it, then print it for completion by hand.
  4. Explain the local setup. The server joins their existing business Wi-Fi, and staff sign in with their own accounts.
  5. Show the right-sized offer. The starter server uses a 32 GB card and provides 20 GB of shared-file space for everyday forms and references.
  6. Explore the next step. If this matches their needs, discuss a pilot. If their needs have grown, use that as the opening for a broader data-management conversation.
Good fit question: “Would it help if your employees could check the time-off calendar and print a blank form from a phone or computer on your business Wi-Fi?”
